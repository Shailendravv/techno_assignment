# On-call escalation policy

Applies to every service. This policy covers **who to wake and when**. It does
not cover what to tell customers — see the incident communication policy for
that.

## Severity levels

| Severity | Definition | Response time |
|---|---|---|
| SEV1 | Complete outage of a customer-facing flow, or any data loss | 5 minutes |
| SEV2 | Degraded customer-facing flow; a workaround exists | 15 minutes |
| SEV3 | Internal impact only; no customer-visible effect | Next business day |

Payments incidents are raised one level above where they would otherwise sit,
because they are regulated and because financial impact is hard to reverse.

## The paging chain

1. **Primary on-call** for the owning team is paged automatically by the alert.
2. If the primary does not acknowledge within **5 minutes**, the page escalates
   to the **secondary on-call** automatically. You do not need to do this by hand.
3. If the secondary does not acknowledge within a further **5 minutes**, the page
   escalates to the **engineering manager** for that team.
4. For SEV1, the **incident commander** rota is paged in parallel from the start,
   not after the chain has been exhausted.

## When to escalate manually

Escalate without waiting for the timer when any of these is true:

- The incident crosses two or more services and no single team owns the fix.
- Mitigation has been attempted and the metric is not recovering.
- You are the only responder and you need someone to take communications while
  you keep working. This is the most commonly skipped reason and the most costly.
- A database migration may need to be reversed.

Escalating early is never held against you. The review asks whether the decision
was reasonable with what was known at the time, not whether it turned out to be
necessary.

## Handover

Any incident open longer than four hours gets a formal handover: current
hypothesis, what has been ruled out, what is currently mitigating, and who has
been told what. Write it in the incident channel, not in a direct message.

## After the incident

Every SEV1 and SEV2 gets a written postmortem within five working days. The
postmortem is blameless and focuses on the conditions that allowed the failure,
not on the individual who triggered it.
